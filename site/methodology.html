Methodology

How every number is defined

Data

All numbers come from nflverse release files read with nflreadr: play-by-play (load_pbp), schedules, rosters, players, weekly player stats and weekly team stats. The pipeline is an R script that runs weekly (Tuesday morning Pacific) and writes static JSON; the site is static HTML.

Play filter

Before anything else, play-by-play is restricted to regular-season plays with a non-missing EPA whose play_type is run or pass. That drops kickoffs, punts, field goals, extra points, kneel-downs, spikes and nullified (no-play) penalties. Team box-score totals therefore differ from official totals by kneel-down yards.

Rushing definitions

  • Team rushing (Section 3a, box scores): rush_attempt == 1, which includes QB scrambles.
  • Designed runs (RB table, all situational stats): rush_attempt == 1 & qb_scramble == 0.
  • Rush share: designed runs ÷ (designed runs + dropbacks), where a dropback is qb_dropback == 1.
  • Explosive run: 10+ yards. Explosive play rate on game pages: 20+ yard pass or 10+ yard run.
  • Success: nflverse success (EPA > 0).

Situations

  • Goal line: yardline_100 ≤ 5 by default, ≤ 10 as a toggle.
  • 3rd and short: down == 3 & ydstogo ≤ 2 by default, ≤ 3 as a toggle. 4th and short uses the same distance.
  • Conversion: first down or touchdown on the play.
  • League rate for situational stats is pooled across all 32 teams (total TDs ÷ total attempts), not the average of team rates.

Ranks

Every stat is computed for all 32 teams from the same filtered play-by-play and then ranked 1–32; ties share the better rank. Each stat carries a higher_is_better flag so defensive stats rank correctly (rank 1 = fewest allowed). Situational ranks require a minimum number of attempts: 10 goal-line rushes and 15 third-and-short rushes over a full season, prorated by weeks completed with a floor of 3, so ranks exist early in the season. Teams below the threshold show "n/a".

Running backs are ranked on yards per game among all RBs/FBs averaging at least 5 designed carries per game. Quarterbacks are ranked among QBs with at least 14 dropbacks per team game played.

Team averages

  • Points from the schedule file. Yards from play-by-play: passing yards are net of sack yardage; rushing yards include scrambles.
  • Red zone trip: a drive with at least one run/pass snap inside the opponent 20; TD if the drive ended in a touchdown.
  • Turnovers: interceptions plus fumbles lost on offensive snaps. Takeaways mirror that from the defense's side. Special-teams turnovers are not counted.
  • Time of possession is summed from nflverse drive data.

Opponent and quarterback

  • Best win / worst loss: among the opponent's wins, the beaten team with the highest current win percentage (W + ½T) ÷ games; among losses, the winner with the lowest. Ties go to more games played, then alphabetical. The record at the time of the game is shown as a secondary line.
  • Division standing: win percentage, then point differential (a simplification of the official tiebreakers).
  • Starting QB: the opponent passer with the most dropbacks in the game; for upcoming games, their most recent starter. A flag shows when that differs from the QB with the most starts this season.
  • Career stats: summed from weekly player stats, regular season, 1999–present. Dropbacks = attempts + sacks. The career record as starter credits, for each team-game, the passer with the most dropbacks. A QB injured on the opening drive is credited with neither a start nor a decision.
  • Prior-season baseline: previous regular season only (no playoffs).
